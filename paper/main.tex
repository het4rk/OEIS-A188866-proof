\title{A proof of Hardin's empirical column formulas for OEIS A188866 via ranges of lazy walks, formalized in Lean~4}
\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Let $T(n,k)$ be the number of $n\times k$ binary arrays without the pattern $0\,1$ vertically, diagonally or antidiagonally (OEIS A188866~\cite{oeis}). R.~H.~Hardin recorded eight column formulas, from $T(n,1)=n+1$ to $T(n,8)=2187n-3495$ for $n>5$, which are labelled ``Empirical'' in the OEIS. We prove the uniform closed form $T(n,k)=3^{k-1}(n+1)-R_{k-1}$ for all $k\ge1$ and $n\ge k-2$. Here $R_L$ is the total range of all $3^L$ lazy walks of length $L$. Evaluating $R_0,\dots,R_7$ yields all eight of Hardin's formulas, each on exactly Hardin's range of validity. The whole development is machine-checked in Lean~4 with Mathlib~\cite{mathlib}: no \texttt{sorry}, and only the axioms \texttt{propext}, \texttt{Classical.choice} and \texttt{Quot.sound}.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
OEIS A188866 (R.~H.~Hardin, 2011) tabulates $T(n,k)$, the number of $n\times k$ binary arrays in which a $0$ at position $(i,j)$ forces $0$ at $(i+1,j-1)$, $(i+1,j)$ and $(i+1,j+1)$ whenever those cells exist. The entry lists the formulas
\begin{align*}
T(n,1)&=n+1, & T(n,2)&=3n+1, & T(n,3)&=9n-1,\\
T(n,4)&=27n-13\ (n>1), & T(n,5)&=81n-65\ (n>2), & T(n,6)&=243n-265\ (n>3),\\
T(n,7)&=729n-987\ (n>4), & T(n,8)&=2187n-3495\ (n>5),
\end{align*}
all marked ``Empirical''. We prove them, together with a single formula that covers every column.

\section{Related work}
The table A188866 and the formulas were contributed to the OEIS by R.~H.~Hardin in 2011~\cite{oeis}. Columns $k=4,\dots,8$ are the separate entries A188861--A188865~\cite{oeiscols}, which repeat the corresponding empirical formulas. A.~Howroyd (2017) gave a bijection between these arrays and strings over $\{0,\dots,n\}$ of length $k$ whose adjacent letters differ by at most one~\cite{howroyd}. These strings are the \emph{smooth words} of Knopfmacher, Mansour, Munagi and Prodinger~\cite{kmmp}, who give their generating function (Theorem~2.2) and an explicit trigonometric formula for their number (Theorem~2.4). \textbf{In principle, therefore, the column formulas follow from Howroyd's bijection combined with~\cite{kmmp}.} Our contribution is a direct, elementary proof that gives a uniform closed form with constant term $R_{k-1}$, the total range of lazy walks, together with what is to our knowledge the first machine-checked verification. Together these turn the OEIS's ``Empirical'' column formulas for $k=1,\dots,8$ into proved results. Neither $R_L$ nor $3^L-R_L$ appeared in an OEIS search on October 12, 2026. The problem is not in the \texttt{formal-conjectures} repository~\cite{fc}, so our Lean definition is transcribed directly from the OEIS entry. The formalization uses Lean~4~\cite{lean4} and Mathlib~\cite{mathlib}.

\section{Statement}
For $s\in\{-1,0,1\}^L$ put $S_0=0$ and $S_i=s_1+\dots+s_i$, and define
\[
R_L=\sum_{s\in\{-1,0,1\}^L}\Bigl(\max_{0\le i\le L}S_i-\min_{0\le i\le L}S_i\Bigr).
\]
Then $R_0,\dots,R_9=0,2,10,40,146,508,1716,5682,18546,59884$.
\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
For all $k\ge1$ and $n\ge k-2$, $T(n,k)=3^{k-1}(n+1)-R_{k-1}$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{corollary}\label{cor:cols}
All eight of Hardin's column formulas hold, each on the range stated in the OEIS.
\end{corollary}

\section{Proof overview}
\textbf{Step 1 (arrays and smooth words).} Taking $j'=j$ shows that each column is a block of $1$s followed by $0$s. Let $t_j\in\{0,\dots,n\}$ be the height of column $j$. The diagonal conditions are then equivalent to $|t_j-t_{j+1}|\le1$. So $T(n,k)=W(n,k-1)$, where $W(M,L)$ counts words $t\in\{0,\dots,M\}^{L+1}$ whose adjacent letters differ by at most one.

\textbf{Step 2 (fibres over step sequences).} A smooth word is determined by its step sequence $s\in\{-1,0,1\}^L$ and its first letter $x$, since $t_i=x+S_i$. The word stays in $[0,M]$ exactly when $x\in[-\min S,\,M-\max S]$. Because $\max S-\min S\le L\le M+1$, this interval contains exactly $M+1-(\max S-\min S)$ integers. In particular it is never too short for the count to be correct, a boundary case that must be handled explicitly. Summing over all $3^L$ step sequences gives $W(M,L)=3^L(M+1)-R_L$.

\textbf{Step 3.} Kernel evaluation of $R_0,\dots,R_7$ gives Corollary~\ref{cor:cols}.

\section{Limitations}
We give no closed form or generating function for $R_L$. Hardin's empirical row recurrences (fixed $n$, varying $k$) are not formalized. They follow from the transfer-matrix and Chebyshev description~\cite{kmmp}. The novelty is modest: the result settles a family of empirical OEIS formulas and identifies their constant term.
